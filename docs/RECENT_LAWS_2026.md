The date, printed law number and Gazette issue are verified from each PDF before
extraction. Every article number must form the full consecutive source sequence.
Article 63 of 5351 has a Latin `o` in its PDF heading; this is recognized without
losing the article or changing the retained printed label. Quoted replacement
article headings remain inside their actual amending article.

Text comes from the original PDFs, with source-page ranges. Cross-page broken
words are joined only when a printed trailing hyphen is followed by a Greek
lowercase continuation. The sampled margin check excluded only
running headers, page numbers and publisher contact details. Tables and special
layouts can always be compared with the preserved original PDF.

Only unambiguous consecutive top-level paragraph sequences become paragraph
units. Numbered paragraphs within quoted replacement provisions remain within
the quotation. The unnumbered publication order and signatures have their own
selectable `closing` body and do not inflate the article count. These original
publications are not represented as later consolidated editions.

Explicit unquoted references to the present law or present article receive exact
self targets. The Income Tax Code alias is fixed to 4172/2013 using the code's
explicit identification in this batch. An uncollected target remains pending;
a nearby article number or quoted reference cannot silently supply a new scope.
Existing citation parsing and graph target resolution remain in use.

`config/recent-laws.json` is the explicit batch manifest. The recent reading
guides use `source_article_count`, separate from the older collection extractor's
`article_count` workflow. Personal annotations and previously collected texts are
preserved. A complete database backup precedes every recent import.
